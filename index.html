From small-business websites to AI agents.

I'm Kirong, a developer in Nairobi. Every chapter of my work started with a real problem someone had, and I kept going until I understood the tools well enough to solve it properly.

  1. Websites that sell

    I began with small Kenyan businesses: a trainer, a thrift shop, a salon. They didn't need anything fancy. They needed a fast page, prices people could see, and a WhatsApp button that turned browsing into a message.

  2. When the site started to talk

    Sites that hand people to WhatsApp worked, but I wanted them to do more. I connected M-Pesa payments and AI models, and Kirong AI began as a simple chat.

  3. Making it real

    The chat became an agent platform: it plans a task, acts one step at a time, and the server, not the model, decides what needs your approval. Shipping it taught me what tutorials skip: serverless limits, a service worker that kept serving stale pages, and payments from people who close the tab mid-checkout.

  4. Opening the black box

    In October 2026 I started Our Engine: a neural network built from a single neuron, then backpropagation, then a small transformer, all written by hand. My rules are simple: predict the output before running it, and never merge code I can't explain. It's an experiment, not a product, and it's how I'm learning what's really inside the AI tools.

StorageVercel Blob and checkpoints
SafetyEvery proposed action is re-classified on the server. Anything above low risk can pause for explicit approval, and decisions are logged.
RuntimeOne action per planning step, bounded step budgets, saved checkpoints, and runs that can resume after an interruption.
ExecutionAllowed code workflows run in an isolated Vercel Sandbox; numeric calculations use a separate bounded evaluator.
AI routingPlanning and chat work is routed across configured providers, so one slow provider doesn't stop the app.
DataPrivate storage for chats, memory, projects, files, research, agent runs and audit records.
KnowledgeUploaded PDF, DOCX and text files are extracted for the agent to use.

Retrieval is bounded text and keyword search over uploaded files, not a vector database. I'd add semantic search when a knowledge base grows large enough to need it.
